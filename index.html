One step instead of twenty-five: notes on UniReflow

Published 15 July 20264 min read

UniReflow is one model that does two jobs: it segments an image, and it generates an image from a segmentation mask. Both happen in a single step. The paper won the Best Paper Award at NCWIA 2026.

The idea in one picture

Teacherflow matching on frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 + LoRA
One reflow passstraightens the trajectories
One-step studenta direction embedding picks image → mask or mask → image

How it works

A flow-matching teacher is trained on frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 with LoRA adapters. One reflow pass straightens its trajectories, and the result is distilled into a student that runs in a single step. A direction embedding tells the student whether to go from image to mask (perception) or from mask to image (generation).

The results

39.2mIoU on COCO-Stuff in one step (SemFlow: 38.6 in 25)
175 mson an RTX 4090, down from 2,775 ms
55.7mIoU on CelebAMask-HQ face parsing

Keeping computer vision running at 18+ airport sites

Published 20 August 20265 min read

Since January 2024 I have been running the license plate recognition and parking-gate camera systems that Alfabeta operates across sites in Indonesia, including more than 18 Intel NUC deployments at airports. This post is about the less glamorous half of computer vision: keeping it running.

The setup

Each site has cameras feeding small Intel NUC machines. The detection tools run in Docker on those boxes, and ETL jobs move the results into MySQL, with Redis alongside. The same kind of stack also runs people counting in busy retail spaces and intruder detection.

18+Intel NUC deployments at airports
95%+people-counting accuracy in busy retail spaces
75%fewer false positives in intruder detection

Reaching boxes you can't touch

The machines sit on different site networks, far from each other. To reach them, I set up a self-hosted Tailscale/Headscale network that puts every edge device on one private network.

Watching everything

Grafana and Prometheus watch the Docker hosts and the pipelines behind each site, so problems show up on a dashboard before they show up in a complaint.

Two frozen backbones for crowd counting

Published 8 September 20264 min read

DuoDiffCount counts people in dense crowds by combining two frozen backbones. It received the Honorable Mention Paper Award (佳作論文獎) at CVGIP 2026 in Tainan.

The idea in one picture

Stable Diffusionfrozen · fine spatial detail
CLIPfrozen · semantic context
Crowd countthe two feature sets, fused

Two backbones, both frozen

Stable Diffusion features hold fine spatial detail, and CLIP features carry semantic context. DuoDiffCount fuses the two to count people in dense crowds. Neither backbone is fine-tuned.
